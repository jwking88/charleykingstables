Biography

One of Harness Racing's Finest

Charley King was one of the top harness horsemen of the 1950s and 60s. Born Charles Lafayette King IV on April 2, 1922 in Rock Castle County, Kentucky, he survived a severe farming accident at 18 that left him in a coma for 29 days — and went on to build one of the most powerful standardbred horse stables of his era.

Starting with a partnership with high school classmate Howard Beissinger and a mare named Ladies First, Charley and his wife Bettie built the Ohio Valley Stables into a powerhouse, campaigning horses like Irvin Paul, Adios Marches, and Argo Kid at the biggest tracks in North America — Yonkers, Roosevelt Raceway, Santa Anita, and Hollywood Park.

Notable Horses

From world-record holders to the stars of television, the Charley King Stables produced legends of the sulky.

1960s · Pacer
Irvin Paul
Three world records. $548,000 in lifetime earnings. Runner-up Standardbred of the Year 1962. The sixth top money-winning pacer in the world.
1950s · Trotter
Ladies First
Charley's first great success — equaled the world record for four-year-old trotting mares and became one of the top earning mares of the 1950s.
1960s · Pacer
Adios Marches
Top free-for-all pacer of the mid-1960s with nearly $200,000 in earnings. Competed in the famed 1966 Pace of the Century at Yonkers.
1950s · Trotter
Annette Sue
Won a record-setting 16 straight races. Combined with Ladies First for over $300,000 in career earnings — more than $2 million in today's money.
1960s · Trotter
Argo Kid
Nearly $150,000 in earnings before his untimely death at age four. Charley believed he had the potential to be one of the best trotters in the world.
